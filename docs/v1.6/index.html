01 / Define the investigation

A question is a starting point.
Not a research specification.

First, agree what should—and should not—be investigated. The assistant separates your observations from possible explanations, proposes a brief, and waits for scope approval before issuing the RB.

The starting point
“

I keep noticing that assistants become unreliable in long chats. Is there any research explaining why?

Normal language. Multiple possible explanations. No clear measurement, population, or evidence standard.

02 / Investigate the agreed question

From an agreed scope
to a qualified answer.

The investigation asks what the evidence supports, what remains uncertain, and where the answer applies. Both routes begin with the issued RB; the instructions supplied to the research session differ.

Recommended for v1.6

Research from the RB alone

Attach the RB to a fresh chat outside the project and request a Markdown report. This session receives the standalone scope, without the full protocol's additional rules.

Full-engine execution

Apply the full research protocol

Execute inside the configured project. The protocol also prescribes appraisal of central evidence, question coverage, a structured companion and checks before delivery.

05 / Why v1.6 recommends RB-first research

Built as a method.
Examined as an experiment.

The October 2026 benchmark compared an informal question, research from an RB alone, and research with the full protocol. Its report-quality results changed the recommended route.

11/12

Models produced better-judged reports from Research Boundaries than from the initial question alone.

Adding the full protocol did not consistently improve those reports further. That is why v1.6 recommends agreeing the scope first, then researching from the RB in a fresh session.

Historical October 2026 conditions; one LLM judge; five shared topics across models. These judgments do not validate the companion or guarantee performance on other questions or later versions.

Inspect the experiment

15 model configurations, hosted and local execution, 1,666 grading sessions and 4,075 pairwise verdicts—with reproducible records and documented limits.

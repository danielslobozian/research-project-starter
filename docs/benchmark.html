02 / What the three arms showed

Scope helped. More protocol was conditional.

The edition separates the benefit of an approved scope from the additional effect of the full research engine.

B COMPARED WITH A · RB-ONLY VS INFORMAL QUESTION

Better judged reports in 11 of 12 models.

The edition found an improvement from defining the RB first for 11 models that ran both arms. Gemma 4 12B showed no measurable difference.

The full engine also beat the informal question for 10 of 12 models. That is a different comparison from asking whether it adds value over the RB alone.

C COMPARED WITH B · FULL ENGINE VS RB-ONLY

3 improved · 7 uncertain · 3 worse

Across 13 model/effort configurations, the full engine improved judged reports for Luna at medium effort, Sonnet and Mistral Medium. Seven configurations had no measurable difference; Sol at low and high effort and Astra performed worse.

These are 12 distinct models, with Sol counted at two effort settings. Haiku and Luna at xhigh ran B only and are excluded from this comparison. “Uncertain” means no measurable difference in the edition, not equivalence.

03 / How reports were judged

One judge. An explicit evaluation process.

Claude Opus 5.5 was the sole LLM judge, using fixed grading rules v4.1. Repeated comparisons make its preferences inspectable; they do not establish agreement among independent judges.

BLINDED COMPARISON

Same question, shuffled reports

Reports were presented without model labels, in an order randomized outside the grader. Within-model sessions compared A, B and C for the same topic and run; other sessions compared two to four reports and returned pairwise verdicts.

BOUNDED EVIDENCE CHECKS

Reasoned preferences

The judge checked a limited sample of cited sources before deciding: first better, second better, mixed, similar or inconclusive. The grader was tested for repeat consistency and detection of deliberately degraded reports before the panel ran.

STATISTICAL UNCERTAINTY

Topics, not thousands of independent trials

The cross-model ranking fits 4,075 pairwise verdicts on five shared topics. Its 95% intervals resample topics. Many verdicts do not remove the uncertainty of a small topic set; nearby ranks should not be read as certain quality gaps.

What this evaluation does and does not establish

The judge’s model family was also evaluated, so model-family preference is a possible bias. Only the Markdown reports were graded; diagrams, the structured JSON companion and every methodological feature were not independently validated. Source spot checks are not exhaustive fact checking. The results describe this edition’s environments and judge preferences.

05 / What it cost

Quality has a compute bill.

A benchmark is not just the final score. It is the cost of running research, grading reports, handling failures, and reproducing the analysis.

GRADING

$1,350

List-price cost of 1,666 grading sessions—not the all-in benchmark cost.

RESEARCH GENERATION

Per-report prices

Model-specific pricing and observed usage. The arm-B ranking above includes RB preparation. Published prices allocate retries and failed attempts to delivered reports.

LOCAL EXECUTION

Energy + search

Local cost estimates use measured GPU energy and Brave Search API charges, not a rented GPU rate.

No combined project total is presented here. Actual bills, list-price calculations and local energy/search estimates use different accounting bases. The edition’s pricing rules and deviations ↗ explain the distinctions, including the price change during the Mistral Large 4 round.

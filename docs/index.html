Open-source research methodology · v1.7b.10

Research that remembers its boundaries.

Two projects for ChatGPT, Claude or Mistral Vibe. The first asks who the report is for, agrees the research with you and issues a Research Boundaries (RB) file. The second takes that file and researches it, and returns a report with its evidence and limits.

01 / Two projects, two decisions

Agree the research first.
Then run it.

Deciding what to investigate and investigating it happen in two separate projects. The RB file is the only thing that passes from one to the other, so the research works from what you approved and nothing else.

Generation project

From your question to an approved RB

Describe what you want to know in your own words. The project asks a few reader questions, then proposes a brief: the main question, the supporting questions, what is in and out of scope, and the evidence expected. Correct it as often as you like; when you write “approved”, it issues the RB as a file. It does not research.

Execution project

From the RB to a report

Open a new chat, attach the RB and send it with no text. The project searches, reads and appraises the sources, answers each question or states the evidence gap, and checks its claims against the sources before delivering. You receive the report as HTML (or PDF if you asked for one), its Markdown source and the RB.

02 / New in 1.7b.10

It asks who the report is for
before it proposes anything.

The first reply is a short set of questions, at most four, each open and each with a stated default. Answer what you like; “skip” keeps the defaults, and the RB records them as assumptions.

What you already know

Sets the depth: a newcomer gets terms explained, an expert gets the statistics.

What the report is for

Decides what leads: a decision, an explanation for others, or a claim to check and cite.

Who will read it

The requester, or the colleagues, manager or committee it will be passed to.

Your situation

Asked only when it changes which evidence applies. It shapes emphasis, not scope, unless you choose to narrow the research.

Questions are skipped when everything is already known, when you waive them, or when you continue or revise an earlier topic. Personal details are written impersonally in the RB and the report.

03 / What you keep

The answer stays tied
to what was agreed.

topic.rb.mdWhat was agreed

The questions, scope, exclusions, evidence expectations, reader context and the approval. Keep it: a later reader can see what the research was meant to answer.

05 / Evidence

Benchmarked at 1.6.
Not yet at 1.7b.10.

The October 2026 benchmark measured engine 1.6. It found that writing the research from an RB gave better-judged reports than writing it from the initial question, for 11 of 12 models. Version 1.7b.10 has not been benchmarked: its reader questions and execution project are shown by the recorded cases, not measured.

11/12

Models produced better-judged reports from Research Boundaries than from the initial question alone (engine 1.6).

October 2026 conditions; one LLM judge; five shared topics across models. The result does not carry over automatically to later versions.

Inspect the experiment

15 model configurations, 1,666 grading sessions and 4,075 pairwise verdicts, with the raw records.

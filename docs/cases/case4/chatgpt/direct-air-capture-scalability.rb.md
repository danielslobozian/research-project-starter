# 1. Research objective and motivation

Assess whether direct air capture (DAC) of carbon dioxide can contribute meaningfully to climate change mitigation at scale, and under what conditions public investment in regional DAC pilot projects would be justified. The investigation must distinguish scientific feasibility, demonstrated operation, and projected large-scale deployment. Small-scale technical success must not be assumed to demonstrate commercial or climate-relevant scalability. Findings should support a funding decision without presupposing that DAC investment is either warranted or unwarranted.

# 2. Primary research question

**Q0.** Under what technical, economic, environmental and institutional conditions could direct air capture achieve a meaningful contribution to climate change mitigation, and what evidence would justify public investment in regional pilot projects?

# 3. Secondary research questions

**Q1.** What DAC technologies exist, what has been demonstrated operationally, and what remains unproven?

**Q2.** What limits expansion to climate-relevant scales, including energy, materials, infrastructure and permanent CO₂ storage?

**Q3.** What are credible current and projected removal costs, and how sensitive are they to technological and economic assumptions?

**Q4.** How much CO₂ is durably removed after accounting for life-cycle emissions, energy requirements and storage risks?

**Q5.** What can pilot projects realistically establish, and what evidence, safeguards and performance milestones should determine whether public funding is warranted?

# 4. Included scope

- International evidence on established and emerging DAC approaches supported by credible research, including documented operation and the uncertainties involved in extrapolating to larger deployment.
- Technical and systemic scalability: energy requirements and low-carbon energy availability, material inputs, enabling infrastructure, and the capacity and permanence of CO₂ storage.
- Current and projected economic performance, including uncertainty in cost estimates and the assumptions driving them.
- Climate effectiveness assessed as **net durable CO₂ removal**, accounting for relevant life-cycle emissions, energy inputs, storage permanence, and storage risks. Distinguish DAC linked to durable storage from CO₂ utilization pathways that do not provide durable removal.
- Relevant environmental trade-offs and institutional factors that condition deployment and public funding.
- The rationale for public pilot funding, the learning or demonstration value of pilots, appropriate safeguards and measurable performance milestones, and criteria for supporting, deferring, or deprioritizing public investment.
- Comparison with emissions prevention and other carbon-removal approaches **to the extent needed** to assess funding opportunity costs, without expanding into a comprehensive review of all mitigation measures.
- Evidence and relevant developments available at the actual research date, including foundational work and subsequent operational evidence.
- A general international appraisal, translated into region-independent decision criteria rather than a judgment about a specific jurisdiction or proposed facility.

# 5. Exclusions

- Detailed process-engineering design or engineering specifications for a DAC plant.
- Procurement analysis, site selection, or investment due diligence for any particular project.
- Jurisdiction-specific permitting, regulatory compliance, or location-specific economic feasibility assessments.
- A comprehensive assessment or ranking of every carbon-removal technology or climate-mitigation option.
- Treating a commercial announcement, nominal capture capacity, or projected removal as equivalent to independently verified, delivered net durable removal.

The report must not generalize international evidence into a claim that a specific region has suitable electricity, infrastructure, storage, policy conditions, or a fundable project without region-specific assessment.

# 6. Research approach and evidence expectations

Use a **structured narrative evidence review**, not an exhaustive systematic review. Seek peer-reviewed research, life-cycle assessments, independently documented operational performance, and authoritative institutional evaluations relevant to each question. Public company disclosures may supply claims or context, but should not be treated as independent performance verification.

Differentiate measured performance, validated engineering estimates, modelled scenarios, commercial plans, and speculative projections. Explain scale and cost metrics and their denominators; distinguish gross CO₂ captured from net, durable CO₂ removed; and compare estimates only where system boundaries, energy assumptions, storage assumptions, and units permit a meaningful comparison. Assess the provenance, relevance, limitations, and uncertainty of major claims, including potential confounding factors when interpreting pilot results or cost trends. Identify material gaps between present operation and large-scale deployment.

For funding implications, assess which propositions could reasonably be tested by a pilot, which require system-level evidence beyond a pilot, and which decision criteria are conditional on local circumstances. Evaluate the evidence consistent with several defensible outcomes, including targeted pilot support, deferral pending specified evidence, or prioritizing alternative climate investments. Present conclusions and recommendations as conditional on evidence, not a command to adopt a specific position. State disagreements, missing data, uncertainty, and limits to generalization. No fixed corpus or source-access restriction has been imposed.

# 7. Status of examples

No scope-defining examples supplied. References to DAC pilots and operational projects designate types of evidence, not mandatory named case studies.

# 8. Deliverable contract

- **Audience/prior knowledge:** Members of a regional climate committee who understand climate science and mitigation basics but are not engineering specialists.
- **Language:** English.
- **Explanation depth:** Accessible and decision-oriented. Explain technical terms in ordinary language and provide the quantitative comparisons, assumptions, and methodological qualifications needed to evaluate costs, scale, net removal, and claims about pilots; avoid unnecessary engineering detail.
- **Purpose/use:** Inform a committee recommendation on whether and under what conditions to fund regional DAC pilots, including evidence thresholds, safeguards, trade-offs, and funding implications.
- **Reader context:** A regional public-funding decision without a specified location; use international evidence and indicate which conclusions would require local verification. Do not assume a country, region, regulatory framework, or particular DAC project.

# 9. Assumptions, uncertainties, and open boundaries

- No jurisdictional preference was specified. The geographical basis is therefore international evidence, with region-independent funding criteria and explicit limits on local applicability.
- No particular pilot, technology vendor, storage location, regional energy mix, budget size, or funding programme is designated as mandatory. These are not to be invented or assumed.
- The assessment concerns whether DAC can matter at climate-relevant scale and what pilots can establish about that prospect; it does not assume that pilots themselves would remove climatically significant quantities of CO₂.
- The ten-page cap, including references, requires concise prioritization of decision-critical evidence rather than exhaustive technical background. No conclusion about DAC's feasibility, costs, or funding suitability is stipulated in advance.

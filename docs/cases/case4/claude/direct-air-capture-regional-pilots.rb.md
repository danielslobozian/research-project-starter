# 1. Research objective and motivation

A regional climate committee must decide whether to fund direct air capture (DAC) pilots. The investigation examines whether DAC with durable storage can remove CO2 at a scale that matters for climate goals, and what that implies for such a decision.

The following premises are to be tested, not assumed: that DAC can reach a meaningful scale; that costs will fall substantially; that a regional pilot would teach something existing projects do not; and that DAC is a good use of limited money and clean electricity.

# 2. Primary research question

**Q0.** Can direct air capture (DAC) with durable storage remove CO2 at a scale that matters for climate goals, and what does that imply for a regional decision on funding pilots?

# 3. Secondary research questions

**Q1.** What counts as "a scale that matters": how much DAC do credible climate scenarios need, and by when?

**Q2.** How does operating capacity and measured performance compare with announced plans and claims?

**Q3.** What are the limits from energy, water, land, materials, transport and storage, and how much CO2 is removed after life-cycle emissions?

**Q4.** What does DAC cost now, how far might costs plausibly fall, and who pays?

**Q5.** How does it compare with other uses of the same money and clean power, including emission cuts and other carbon removal methods?

**Q6.** What can pilots actually teach, what have earlier ones shown, and what should a regional funder check first?

# 4. Included scope

All main DAC technologies; global evidence; foundational work plus developments up to the search date. Other uses of the same money and clean power, including emission cuts and other carbon removal methods, are included as comparators under Q5. "Funding pilots" covers both hosting a pilot and co-funding a project elsewhere (see section 9).

# 5. Exclusions

- Point-source capture and other removal methods, except as comparators.
- Engineering design.
- Site-specific feasibility or financial modelling.
- Legal and procurement advice.
- Ranking of companies.

# 6. Research approach and evidence expectations

A structured narrative evidence review, not a systematic one. The review will favour peer-reviewed work and public agency data, flag operator figures as self-reported, separate measured results from modelled ones, and give ranges with confidence levels. Any question the evidence cannot settle will be marked as unresolved. No fixed corpus applies.

# 8. Deliverable contract

- Audience/prior knowledge: Members of a regional climate committee who know climate basics but not engineering.
- Language: English.
- Explanation depth: The intuitive idea first, then the technical detail needed to follow and judge the evidence, with technical terms explained in plain words; formulas or statistics only where a finding depends on them.
- Purpose/use: Support a recommendation to the committee on whether to fund DAC pilots. The report sets out the conditions under which funding is or is not justified, rather than issuing a flat instruction.
- Reader context: Assumed: no specific region, with the report showing which local conditions change the answer; a modest budget that cannot pay for a commercial-scale plant alone.

- The 10-page maximum is assumed to include the summary and references, leaving about 8 pages of body. This assumption was adopted from an open question in BR-001.
- "Funding pilots" is assumed to include co-funding a project elsewhere as well as hosting one. This assumption was adopted from an open question in BR-001.
- No specific region was given. The reader context assumes none, and the report shows which local conditions change the answer.
- The size and form of the funding were not given. The reader context assumes a modest budget that cannot pay for a commercial-scale plant alone.

# 1. Research objective and motivation

A regional climate committee is considering whether to fund direct-air-capture (DAC) pilot projects. DAC extracts CO2 from ambient air for storage or use, and is promoted as a way to remove carbon at a scale relevant to climate goals. Whether it can actually work at such a scale is not yet established.

This investigation tests, rather than assumes: (a) that DAC can scale beyond today's small demonstration plants to climate-relevant volumes; (b) that it delivers genuinely net removal once the energy it consumes, the materials and equipment it requires, and the fate of the captured CO2 are counted; and (c) that funding pilot projects is a meaningful step toward that scale. The report informs the funding decision; it does not command adoption. Recommendations are phrased as conditional options tied to evidence.

# 2. Primary research question

**Q0.** Can direct air capture of CO2 operate at a scale that materially affects atmospheric CO2 and climate goals?

# 3. Secondary research questions

**Q1.** How does direct air capture work in engineering terms, and what does it require (energy, land, water, materials, sorbent manufacture)?

**Q2.** What is the current and announced deployment scale of direct air capture, compared with the scale considered climate-relevant?

**Q3.** What do current and projected costs per tonne of CO2 captured look like, and how credible are the cost-reduction pathways?

**Q4.** What dependencies and integrity risks (geological storage availability, clean energy supply, net-removal accounting, verification) constrain deployment at a meaningful scale?

**Q5.** What does the evidence imply for a committee deciding whether to fund direct-air-capture pilots, including a brief comparison with alternative uses of the same funds and the conditions under which pilot funding makes sense?

# 4. Included scope

Included:

- Engineered DAC pathways: liquid-solvent systems, solid-sorbent systems, and emerging electrochemical routes.
- Resource requirements: energy, land, water, materials, and sorbent manufacture.
- Costs per tonne of CO2: current and projected, and the bases of cost-reduction claims.
- Deployment: actually operating plants and announced project pipeline, relative to climate-relevant benchmark scales.
- System dependencies: CO2 transport and geological storage, and clean energy supply.
- Benchmark scales: removal volumes recognized in IPCC and IEA net-zero pathway assessments, benchmarked around 1 Gt CO2 per year globally by mid-century.
- The funding decision: evidence bearing on whether and under what conditions a regional committee should fund DAC pilots, with a brief comparison against alternative uses of the same funds.

Time range: foundational work plus developments available at the actual search date, emphasizing recent developments. No fixed corpus; public sources are searched within this scope.

# 5. Exclusions

- Point-source carbon capture from flue gas (CCS), except where a comparison clarifies DAC's distinct position.
- Nature-based removal (for example afforestation) and other carbon-removal methods (for example BECCS, enhanced weathering), except as brief benchmarks for the funding comparison in Q5.
- Detailed regional siting of plants or storage: no specific region is included.
- General climate science that the committee already knows (climate basics are assumed).

# 6. Research approach and evidence expectations

- Review type: structured narrative evidence review; not an exhaustive or systematic review.
- Claim-appropriate evidence: every claim tied to inspected public sources, including peer-reviewed studies, IPCC and IEA assessments, national-laboratory and independent cost analyses, and primary data from operating plants.
- Comparison and appraisal: contested figures, notably cost per tonne, are to be presented as ranges with their underlying bases; demonstrated, projected, and claimed performance are to be kept distinct throughout.
- Handling of uncertainty: uncertainty is stated openly; where evidence is thin or contested, the report says so rather than resolving it by assumption.
- Access limits: web access is available; no fixed corpus restricts searching. Sources that cannot be accessed are named as limitations, not silently replaced.
- Closure expectations: the investigation ends complete, or constrained-incomplete with every pending or blocked question named.

# 9. Assumptions, uncertainties, and open boundaries

- "A scale that matters" is operationalized as removal volumes recognized in IPCC and IEA net-zero pathway assessments, benchmarked around 1 Gt CO2 per year globally by mid-century. This assumption was adopted from open brief question 1, which stayed unanswered.
- The Q5 comparison with alternatives is a brief comparison for context, not a portfolio analysis. Adopted from open brief question 2, which stayed unanswered.
- No additional HTML reading copy: the PDF is the only reader artifact. Adopted from open brief question 3, which stayed unanswered.
- Reader context is None: the requesting user stated no regional preference, so evidence is weighed globally without a specific region.
- Developments after the actual search date at execution are outside the scope.

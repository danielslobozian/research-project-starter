The methodology · engine v1.6

From a question to a defensible investigation.

Agree the questions, exclusions and evidence expectations in a scope conversation. An issued Research Boundaries (RB) file preserves that scope and its approval basis. Research from it in a fresh session, or apply the full protocol inside the project.

01 / From question to investigation

Agree the scope.
Then investigate it.

The proposed brief is a scope proposal. The issued RB is its standalone record. Approval of that scope and authorization to begin substantive research are separate decisions.

01
Ask

Describe an informal research concern.

02
Clarify

Distinguish observations, explanations and examples.

03
Approve

Agree the proposed brief and boundaries.

04
Issue RB

Produce the standalone, identified Markdown contract.

05
Research

Authorize research, usually in a fresh session at the model’s highest available reasoning effort.

06
Preserve

Keep the exact scope, qualified answer and its evidence together.

Before the first brief, the assistant checks host capabilities and may make limited terminology searches. Substantive research begins only after the RB is issued and execution is authorized. Explicitly waiving scope approval produces an RB marked as unapproved assumptions.

02 / Execution paths

Two paths, one starting point.

The normal route begins with an approved RB. Choose whether to supply that scope alone to a new session or also apply the full protocol.

Approval alone—for example, “Approved.”—issues and links the RB, then stops before substantive research. Approval plus a request to start—for example, “Approved, execute.”—issues the RB and authorizes research under the full protocol. These phrases express intent; they are not exact-match commands.

Default for engine 1.6

Fresh-session research · RB-only

Inside the research project:
“Approved.”

Then, in a new chat outside the project:
“Carry out the research this file describes and write the report as a Markdown file.”

Save and attach the issued RB to the new session. Select the highest available reasoning effort. The new session receives the scope, not the full protocol’s evidence, companion or publication rules.

Additional structure

Full research engine

Inside the configured research project:
“Approved, execute.”

Or, once an RB has been issued:
“Execute this RB.”

This route prescribes central-evidence appraisal, counterevidence, coverage tracking, a report and JSON companion, and delivery checks. A PDF is included by default when the host can render it and the accepted presentation includes it. Adherence is not guaranteed.

Why the default? The October 2026 edition found better-judged RB-only reports than informal-question reports for 11 of 12 models tested with both. Adding the full protocol did not consistently improve them further. One LLM judge assessed reports; the companion and every protocol behavior were not independently validated. 03 / The answer and its analytical record

Keep the scope.
Understand the outputs.

The report explains the answer and its limits. Under full-engine execution, the companion retains a structured view of the same investigation so later readers or tools can inspect how its questions, findings and evidence relate.

topic.rb.mdResearch Boundaries

The exact input scope: accepted questions, inclusions, exclusions, evidence expectations and approval basis. It records intended research, not completed findings.

Before research
topic.mdResearch report

The standalone explanation of findings, supporting evidence and limitations. Both routes request it; the full protocol specifies its structure and publication checks.

Both paths
topic.analysis.jsonStructured companion

Accepted questions and qualified answers, relevant findings, supporting, challenging and qualifying sources, access limits and recorded corrections. Read it alongside the report and evidence.

Full engine

Distinguish an inconclusive answer from unfinished work.

The full protocol records both coverage—what was investigated—and the answer that the evidence supports. A question can be fully investigated yet remain inconclusive. Work left undone, or blocked because necessary evidence could not be inspected, must stay visible as unfinished. A search that found no adequate support is limited to the search actually performed.

The report explains those limits and why research stopped. Working checkpoints preserve performed work during execution, separately from the public report and companion. They are pointers back to evidence, not proof that a claim is correct.

Keep scope changes separate from corrected findings.

A changed research question requires a new RB revision. Correcting a finding revises the affected report and companion while preserving the original scope. The companion records what changed and which earlier records are affected; it cannot update every downloaded copy.

What is demonstrated here: the four A/B runs are RB-only and supply no full-engine JSON companion. Its intended value is specified in v1.6, not independently established by report grading. A companion alone cannot prove approval, evidence support, completeness or latestness; check the exact RB, report and original sources when relying on a disputed claim.

04 / Set up engine v1.6

Start without
installing software.

Use a ChatGPT or Claude Project with instructions and uploaded files. Paste the instructions file; upload the protocol and RB template. Those three files configure the method. The explanatory guide is optional reading.

  1. Create a project and open its instructions and files settings.
  2. Paste the complete research-project-instructions.txt into project instructions.
  3. Upload research-protocol.md and research-boundaries-template.md as project files.
  4. Open a new project chat and describe the question you want to investigate.

05 / Honest operating limits

A research method, not a guarantee.

The protocol cannot force a model to follow instructions. Tools, source access and file generation vary by host. It requires honest provenance and treats retrieved sources as evidence rather than instructions; those rules are not a guarantee of correctness or security.
